Stone garden pavilions and gazebos, made to measure

Bonomelli Estate pavilions are neoclassical garden temples, rotundas and orangeries: true stone gazebos designed for the grounds of a villa, an estate or a hotel. Every concept is shaped around its site: Brenda Bonomelli adapts it to the measurements, the light and the views of the garden, and develops it by private commission with licensed architects and partner craftsmen.

Materials: travertine, Sardinian breccia, wrought iron and glass

The structure is in natural stone, travertine or Sardinian breccia, with hand-forged iron elements and tempered glass. The glass domes bring light inside and turn the pavilion into a garden lounge, a library, a winter garden or a room for receptions and villa weddings.

A modular system, assembled dry

Each pavilion is designed as a set of prefabricated stone modules, joined by steel pins and assembled dry: no long building site in the garden. The pieces arrive ready and are set in sequence; the dome, itself a module, goes on last.

How a pavilion comes to life

1. The concept: renderings and video of the pavilion in its garden. 2. Development with licensed architects and partner craftsmen, including checks and permits. 3. The choice of materials and a detailed quote. 4. Production, transport and installation of the modules, supervised by the studio. Frequently asked questions

Do I need a permit for a stone pavilion in my garden?

It depends on the municipality, the size and any landscape or heritage restrictions on the property. A permanent garden structure usually needs a building permit under Italian rules: a licensed professional checks this before production. Bonomelli Estate works with licensed architects who handle this step.

How much does a bespoke stone pavilion cost?

It depends on the model, the size, the chosen stone and the site. After the concept, the studio prepares a detailed quote covering production, transport and installation.

What is the difference between a pavilion and a gazebo?

A gazebo is usually a light, open structure. A stone pavilion is a small, solid and lasting garden building, often enclosed with glass and crowned by a dome: it can be used in every season.

Travertine or Sardinian breccia: which to choose?

Travertine has warm, light tones, typical of classical Italian gardens; Sardinian breccia has a bolder, more colourful pattern. The choice is made at the materials stage, weighing climate, the style of the house and upkeep with the craftsmen.

Can a pavilion be used for weddings and events?

Yes: several models, such as CAELO and MAGNOLIA, are designed precisely for receptions, private dinners and weddings at villas and hotels.

Do you only work around Verona?

The studio is in San Bonifacio, near Verona, but concepts are adapted to villas, estates and hotels throughout Italy and abroad.
